# Adversarial audit of the TR26-043 v0 true-filtration reconstruction

**Audit date:** 2026-08-21  
**Audited report:** [`research_cycle_02/tr26_043_true_filtration_audit.md`](../research_cycle_02/tr26_043_true_filtration_audit.md)  
**Scope:** source fidelity, the two reveal policies, exact finite claims, filtration-atom grouping, first block exhaustion, reachable subsets, the multiscale reconstruction, and the accompanying script/schema/certificates.  
**Independence:** I did not use the proposer's conclusions as premises. I re-read the two ECCC PDFs, derived the displayed witnesses directly, ran the proposer suite, and separately enumerated complete balanced colorings and used tie branches before comparing outputs.

## Verdict

**Overall: FAIL pending small but required corrections. The core two-block mathematical findings PASS.**

The eager and query-minimal counterexamples, all four smallest-even-`n` classifications, the exact eager atom counts, the reachable-prefix-pair result, and the first-exhaustion residual law all survived independent checking. The failure verdict is for source/status and artifact-interface defects, not for a false counterexample:

1. the report omits the current ECCC Revision 1, which is an explicit retraction note acknowledging the same conditional-filtration gap;
2. the complete-path atom table and `9,664` terminal-atom count are not labelled as eager-policy counts, even though the corresponding query-minimal counts differ;
3. the final summary's “smallest active witness is `n=4`” needs the eager qualifier (`n=6` is the query-minimal minimum);
4. the JSON schema and transition table disagree on the `SCALE_START` phase and are not directly compatible with the enumerator's serialized traces;
5. the multiscale audit correctly finds the missing lower size bound, but should also record that the source's stated logarithmic implication is slightly false even if that lower bound were assumed.

No proposer or canonical artifact was edited in this audit.

## 1. Primary-source fidelity and current status

### 1.1 Original version

The report's original-version source is exact:

- Deepanshu Kush, *An Unconditional Barrier for Proving Multilinear Algebraic Branching Program Lower Bounds*, [ECCC TR26-043 original version](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/043/download), April 1, 2026.
- Independently downloaded SHA-256: `b72a0eedb80aea50dd3b9ed5c1e69cfd3a717fab7225ec69f46cfa6c15ecda0f`, identical to the report and machine-readable artifacts.

- Lemma 3.3 claims `D(t)=a_t-b_t` is a martingale and applies Azuma over the full `t<=m` path. Its proof explicitly says that the two next values have not been revealed and are exchangeable.

## 2. Eager versus query-minimal semantics

The source does not give a query protocol. “Revealed” is part of its probabilistic proof, while the builder is responding to a coloring. Consequently there is no uniquely source-defined reveal order. The proposer's two labelled operational readings are the right conservative treatment:

- **Eager:** literally evaluate both candidate extensions before choosing, including at `H=0`; retain the inspected, unconsumed candidate.
- **Query-minimal:** at `H=0`, use the algebraic fact that both candidate absolute heights equal one, toss the required tie coin first, and inspect only the selected candidate. At nonzero `H`, both candidates still have to be evaluated.

Eager is the closer reading of Definition 3.1's instruction to “compute” the two candidates. Query-minimal is a semantically equivalent implementation for the produced chain, but a strictly coarser observation policy at zero height. Calling both “text-compatible” is acceptable only with this qualification. If one instead treats the builder as knowing all of `f`, the available filtration is still finer, not one on which the v0 fresh-pair calculation becomes valid.

The state correctly retains assigned identities/order as well as colors and coins. This is essential: the adaptive choice of which identity was assigned also conveys information about inspected values.

**Result: PASS.**

## 3. Independent derivation of the smallest counterexamples

### 3.1 Forced probability, eager policy

For `n=8`, condition on the first left and right candidates both being `+1` and on the first tie selecting left. The boundary atom has mass

\[
\frac12\frac37\frac12=\frac3{28}.
\]

After left is consumed, the unconsumed right frontier is a known `+1`. Of the six genuinely unexposed values, two are plus. The next upward probability is therefore `2/6=1/3>1/4`. This also gives

\[
\mathbf E[3^{|H_2|}\mid\mathcal F_1]
=\frac13\,9+\frac23\,1=\frac{11}{3}>3.
\]

For the same atom at `n=2k`,

\[
p_\uparrow=\frac{k-2}{2k-2},\qquad
\mathbf E[\Delta D]=\frac{k}{2k-2}.
\]

The first quantity exceeds `1/4` exactly for `k>=4`, proving the `n=8` threshold for this family without relying on the enumerator. Independent exhaustive checks find no other eager violation for `n=2,4,6`.

**Minimum `n=8`: PASS.**

### 3.2 Forced probability, query-minimal policy

For `n=10`, take the following history:

1. the certain zero-height tie chooses left (`1/2`);
2. reveal and consume `f(1)=+1` (`1/2`);
3. at height one reveal `f(2)=+1` (`4/9`) and `f(6)=+1` (`3/8`);
4. the resulting tie chooses left (`1/2`).

Its mass is `1/48`. At the next boundary, `H=2`, element 6 is a persistent known plus, and the seven unexposed positions contain two pluses. Thus `p_up=2/7>1/4`. Independent enumeration gives maximum values

| `n` | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| query-minimal maximum `p_up` | ineligible | 0 | `1/10` | `1/4` | `1/3` |

so there is no smaller positive even violation.

**Minimum `n=10`: PASS.**

### 3.3 Block-deviation martingale

The independently checked minima are:

| Claim being tested | eager | query-minimal | Direct reason at the minimum |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| literal full Definition-3.1 path | `n=2` | `n=2` | after one half is exhausted, the next block is forced |
| both blocks active at current boundary | `n=4` | `n=6` | a persistent revealed frontier destroys left/right exchangeability |
| every possible next child also has both blocks active | `n=6` | `n=6` | the nonzero-height comparison has necessarily inspected both frontiers |

For eager `n=4`, the atom `f(1)=f(3)=+1`, first tie left, has mass `1/12`; balance forces the new left frontier negative, so the next action is left and `E[Delta D|F_1]=1`. For the strict eager `n=6` atom, the corresponding drift is `3/4`.

The final bullet in Section 9 of the audited report says simply that “the smallest active witness is `n=4`.” It must say **“the smallest eager active witness is `n=4`; the query-minimal active minimum is `n=6`.”** Earlier sections already make this distinction correctly.

**Mathematical minima: PASS. Summary wording: FAIL until qualified.**

## 4. Independent filtration-atom enumeration

I used a separate complete-coloring calculation, not the proposer's deferred-decisions transition routine:

1. enumerate all `binom(n,n/2)` complete balanced colorings;
2. recursively branch only on tie coins actually used by the selected reveal policy;
3. at every chain-step boundary group trajectory mass by
   `(a,b,H,assigned_order,revealed_element_value_map,used_tie_string)`;
4. aggregate child mass within each parent atom to compute conditional upward probability and `D` drift.

The excursion fields in the proposer key do not spuriously refine the partition: they are deterministic functions of the recorded height/assignment history. A fixed left-then-right chain-rule reveal order likewise introduces no boundary refinement. No duplicate keys appeared at a level.

## 5. Reachable subsets

Every two-block probe set has the form

\[
C(a,b)=\{1,\ldots,a\}\cup\{k+1,\ldots,k+b\},\quad 0\leq a,b\leq k.
\]

Independent enumeration under each reveal policy gives, for every even `n<=10`, all grid points at some boundary. At level `t`, the counts are

\[
1,2,\ldots,k,k+1,k,\ldots,2,1,
\]

and their union contains `(k+1)^2` subsets. The `n=10` certificate lists exactly the 36 expected prefix pairs, with no omission or extra set.

For the probe stopped at first exhaustion, the 35 reported subsets mean all subsets visited **before or at** stopping, not 35 distinct terminal subsets. That statement is correct: they are all prefix pairs except `[10]`. There are only 10 distinct terminal assigned subsets (the two sides times five possible residual sizes), which the report does not claim otherwise.

This remains a finite result through `n=10`; the report correctly does not promote it to an all-`n` theorem.

**Result: PASS.**

## 6. First-exhaustion law

An independent stop-on-first-exhaustion calculation gives:

| Reveal policy | active atom counts by local step (final zero included) | terminal filtration atoms |
|---|---|---:|
| eager | `1,8,24,88,264,788,1560,2832,2572,0` | `9,664` |
| query-minimal | `1,4,24,72,264,652,1560,2132,2572,0` | `9,488` |

Both terminal partitions have total mass one. Despite the different observation partitions, both policies induce the same chain/path distribution and hence the same residual-size law:

| residual | probability |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | `3679/16128` |
| 2 | `443/1792` |
| 3 | `1867/8064` |
| 4 | `11/56` |
| 5 | `65/672` |

The numerators on denominator `16128` sum to

`3679 + 3987 + 3734 + 3168 + 1560 = 16128`.

The odd mass is

\[
\frac{3679+3734+1560}{16128}=\frac{997}{1792}.
\]

The invariant residual distribution is expected: at zero height the two policies use the same fair block choice; eager merely observes an extra unselected color. Observation atoms refine differently without changing the full-coloring path law.

Required corrections:

- Section 7 must say **“under eager comparison, `n=10` reaches 9,664 terminal filtration atoms.”**
- `certificates/tr26_043_true_filtration_counterexamples.json` must add `"reveal_policy": "eager"` to `n_10_first_block_exhaustion` and should record the query-minimal count `9,488` separately.
- The complete-path atom table needs the same eager label.
- A minimal-policy first-exhaustion regression would prevent this ambiguity; the current test checks only the default eager output.

The residual table and odd-mass conclusion need no correction.

**Stopping distribution: PASS. Unqualified terminal-atom count: FAIL until labelled.**

## 7. Multiscale reconstruction

### 7.1 Odd recursive residual: confirmed, including at recursive size

The report's `n=10` computation proves only that residual parity is not invariant; all such residuals are below `M0=700`. The underlying under-specification is nevertheless real at recursive sizes, by an explicit family.

Let `n=2k` with odd `k>=701`. Color the left half `+,-,+,-,...,+`. Give the right half total imbalance `-1` and make its first element `+`. At every zero-height tie choose left. At height one the next left `-` is strictly better than the persistent right `+`; at zero the next left `+` can again be selected by the tie. Thus the left block exhausts while the right index remains zero, leaving the entire right half as a residual of odd size `k>=701`. The coloring is globally balanced and the required finite tie sequence has positive probability. The same produced path is possible under both reveal policies.

Definition 3.1/Definition 7.1 do not specify how to split this residual into equal halves. `U1` is therefore a genuine undefined recursive transition, not merely a small-`n` diagnostic.

### 7.2 Gap filler: correct nondeterminism, not a theorem refutation

Lemma 6.1 is existential and its zero-height case permits any next element. No canonical filler is selected. Treating exact emitted output chains as a nondeterministic relation is faithful. However, this is an under-specification only for a unique executable stochastic process: the set system includes all gap subsets, and a fixed choice rule could be added without changing that coverage argument. The audited report already makes essentially this distinction and should retain it.

### 7.3 Small initial sizes: operational omission only

The scale invariant requires `m'_j>=M0/2`, yet v0 says all invariant clauses hold initially merely because `m'_0=n`. For `n<350`, that is false as an operational statement. A finite-size brute-force case can repair an asymptotic theorem, so this observation is not by itself a refutation of the intended asymptotic conclusion. The report states this conservatively.

### 7.4 Next-scale effective-size guard: confirmed, with one extra arithmetic defect

At first block exhaustion the deviation magnitude equals the residual length. Lemma 3.3's event supplies

\[
m_{j+1}\leq 4\sqrt{m'_j}\ln m'_j,
\]

an upper bound. Case A later invokes the unsupported lower bound

\[
m_{j+1}\geq (m'_j)^{2/3}/2
\]

to control the descent and preserve `m'_{j+1}>=M0/2`. The direction mismatch identified as `U4` is correct.

There is also a small independent algebraic defect in the implication as printed. Even assuming that lower bound,

\[
\frac32\ln m_{j+1}+1
\geq \ln m'_j-\frac32\ln2+1
=\ln m'_j-0.03972\ldots,
\]

which does **not** imply `ln m'_j <= (3/2) ln m_{j+1}+1`. The additive constant would need to be at least `(3/2)ln 2`, approximately `1.03972`, absent a stronger lower bound or an integer-rounding proof. This should be added alongside `U4`; it is secondary to the wholly missing lower bound.

**Multiscale under-specification diagnosis: PASS, with the additional constant correction above.**

## 8. Script, schema, certificates, and links

### 8.1 Executable checks

The command

```text
python -B experiments/test_tr26_043_true_filtration.py -v
```

passes all eight tests. Independent complete-coloring enumeration agrees with the proposer on all substantive values checked above. The deferred-decisions code:

- retains a persistent unconsumed frontier;
- branches fresh reveals by exact remaining sign counts using `Fraction`;
- samples only used ties;
- handles eager and query-minimal zero-height semantics as documented;
- stops the multiscale probe at the first child with either block inactive;
- conserves exact mass.

**Result: PASS.**

### 8.2 Schema/transition consistency

All JSON artifacts parse successfully, and all local links in the report resolve. They are not, however, one validated serialization pipeline:

1. `tr26_043_v0_transition_system.json` has transitions from/to `SCALE_START`, but the schema's `phase` enum omits `SCALE_START`. It includes `RECURSE`, for which the transition table supplies no corresponding transition.
2. The schema's frontier object permits `REVEALED_UNCONSUMED` without a `value` and permits `UNREVEALED` with a `value`. These cases should be separated into status-dependent alternatives.
3. The enumerator's actual trace uses event names and shapes such as lowercase `reveal`, `tie_coin_before_color_reveal`, `decision_and_consume`, and excursion events. The schema accepts uppercase `REVEAL`, `TIE_COIN`, `CONSUME`, and `PHASE_CHANGE` only. No serializer or example instance connects the two.
4. The schema does not enforce several semantic invariants (`n` even, `assigned_set=set(assigned_order)`, `H` equal to the assigned-color sum, phase/reason consistency). Some require checks beyond ordinary JSON Schema, but they should be documented as external invariants.

Thus the schema is a useful structural design note, not presently a validator for enumerator outputs or certificates. Either align the event/state serialization and add an instance-validation test, or explicitly label the schema as non-executable pseudostructure.

**Result: FAIL as an integrated machine-checkable contract.**

### 8.3 Certificate metadata

The counterexample certificate's arithmetic matches independent checks. Required metadata repairs are:

- add the Revision-1 retraction status and primary link;
- explicitly label the first-exhaustion block as eager and add the minimal atom count;
- rename or annotate `lemma_4_1_minimal_certificate` and `lemma_3_3_active_minimal_certificate`, which are **eager smallest-`n`** certificates and are easily confused with the adjacent query-minimal policy;
- label the eager scan/table as such wherever extracted independently of its enclosing key.

The `n=10` reachable-subset certificate is exact. Its verification command defaults to eager, but the listed subset family is also independently correct under query-minimal semantics.

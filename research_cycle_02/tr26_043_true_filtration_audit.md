# TR26-043 v0: true-filtration process reconstruction and exact counterexamples

## Status and scope

**Status:** exact finite enumeration plus elementary parametric derivation;
internally `ADVERSARIALLY REVIEWED`; `UNFORMALIZED`; not externally reviewed.
The initial audit and post-correction disposition are recorded in
[`cycle02_true_filtration_adversarial.md`](../audits/cycle02_true_filtration_adversarial.md)
and [`cycle02_true_filtration_disposition.md`](../audits/cycle02_true_filtration_disposition.md).

**Scope:** this audit concerns the conditional probability assertions used in Lemma 4.1 and in the proof of Lemma 3.3 of the original version of TR26-043. It also reconstructs the paper's two-block process and the intended multiscale control flow. It does **not** establish that the numerical tail conclusion of Lemma 3.3 is false, does not decide whether another filtration or argument can prove useful tail estimates, and makes **no claim about O01** or the unrestricted balanced-chain problem.

This reconstruction was made directly from the original primary PDF, without reading Cycle-2 reconstructions by other agents:

- Deepanshu Kush, *An Unconditional Barrier for Proving Multilinear Algebraic Branching Program Lower Bounds*, [ECCC TR26-043 v0](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/043/download), April 1, 2026.
- Downloaded PDF SHA-256: `b72a0eedb80aea50dd3b9ed5c1e69cfd3a717fab7225ec69f46cfa6c15ecda0f`.
- Relevant source locations: Definition 3.1 and Lemma 3.3 (pp. 11--12), Lemma 4.1 and Remark 4.2 (pp. 12--13), Definition 5.1 (p. 13), Definitions 7.1--7.4 (pp. 19--20), and the multiscale strategy (pp. 21--24).

The current official ECCC Revision 1, dated May 11, 2026, is a one-page
retraction note. It acknowledges the conditional-filtration gap in Lemma 4.1
and states that all results in the paper rely on that lemma. Nothing in this
audit reinstates a withdrawn theorem.

## Executive verdict

On the true filtration generated by every color inspected by the strategy and every used tie coin:

1. **Lemma 4.1's conditional bound is false.** Under the literal eager-comparison reading, its smallest positive even counterexample is `n=8` (initial segment `[n]`), where a filtration atom of probability `3/28` has
   \[
   \Pr\bigl(|H(2)|=|H(1)|+1\mid\mathcal F_1^{\rm true}\bigr)=\frac13>\frac14.
   \]
   Exhaustive exact enumeration finds no violation at `n=2,4,6`; the maxima are respectively ineligible/zero, zero, and `1/4`.

   A query-minimal implementation may notice at `H=0` that a tie is certain, toss the coin, and inspect only the selected candidate. Under that more favorable reading Lemma 4.1 still fails, first at `n=10`; all smaller positive even `n` were checked.

2. **The martingale assertion inside the proof of Lemma 3.3 is false.** Read literally over the complete Definition-3.1 path, it first fails at `n=2` after one block is exhausted. Under eager comparison and restricted to the intended phase in which both blocks are active, it first fails at `n=4`: on an atom of probability `1/12`,
   \[
   \mathbb E[D(2)-D(1)\mid\mathcal F_1^{\rm true}]=1.
   \]
   If one uses query-minimal reveals, or additionally insists that both blocks remain active after every possible next choice, the smallest active example is `n=6`.

3. The omitted information is exactly the **inspected but unconsumed frontier**. It persists into the next step. Thus the current two candidates are not generally an exchangeable fresh pair conditional on the actual history.

4. Under eager comparison, the reported `n=10` phenomenon is part of a simple family. For `n=2k`, reveal the first element of each half to be `+1` and let the first tie coin choose the left block. At the next boundary,
   \[
   p_{\uparrow}=\frac{k-2}{2k-2}=\frac{n-4}{2(n-2)},\qquad
   \mathbb E[\Delta D]=\frac{k}{2k-2}=\frac{n}{2(n-2)}.
   \]
   At `n=10`, these are `3/8` and `5/8`. The upward probability approaches `1/2`, rather than `1/4`.

5. The v0 text does not define a unique total multiscale transition system: odd recursive residuals have no equal-half split rule, and Lemma 6.1 supplies an existential but noncanonical gap-filler. The machine-readable reconstruction therefore treats the multiscale machine as a **partial, nondeterministic transition relation** instead of silently inventing conventions.

## 1. What the source claims

For an even segment `I` of size `m`, split `I` into equal left and right halves. At local indices `(a,b)`, the current set is the union of the length-`a` left prefix and length-`b` right prefix. If both blocks are active, Definition 3.1 compares the two possible new absolute global imbalances and consumes the better candidate; an exact tie is resolved by a fresh fair coin. If one block is exhausted, the other is extended.

Write

- `C_t` for the assigned probe set after `t` consumes;
- `H_t=f(C_t)` and `h_t=|H_t|`;
- `D_t=a_t-b_t`.

The proof of Lemma 3.3 asserts that, conditional on the revealed values and coins through the preceding step, the two next candidate values are exchangeable and hence `E[Delta D_t | history]=0`. Lemma 4.1 asserts, whenever `h_t>=1` and both blocks are active,

\[
p_t:=\Pr(h_{t+1}=h_t+1\mid\mathcal F_t)\leq\frac14.
\]

The proof counts all same-sign elements in the global unassigned pool and treats both frontiers as two fresh draws without replacement.

There is a filtration mismatch in the source text itself. Lemma 4.1 calls `F_t` the sigma-algebra of all **revealed** values and coins through step `t`; Definition 5.1 then informally describes only values of elements **assigned** by step `t`. The strategy cannot make its choice without inspecting both candidates. One candidate is not assigned and remains at the frontier, but its value has already been inspected. The requested true filtration must retain it.

## 2. Exact true-filtration state

At the chain-step boundary after `t` consumes and before any new frontier inspection, a complete state contains:

- the global balanced-coloring posterior: every revealed element/value and the exact remaining plus/minus counts;
- the assigned order and assigned subset `C_t`;
- global `H_t` and `h_t`;
- current scale `j`, segment `I_j`, start indices `(a_0,b_0)`, current `(a,b)`, and `D=a-b`;
- each active frontier's identity and status (`UNREVEALED` or `REVEALED_UNCONSUMED`);
- every tie outcome already used;
- the last visit to `h<=1` and any active excursion above that band;
- the multiscale phase (`STEER`, `BLOCK_EXHAUSTED`, `DESCENT`, `GAP_FILL`, `RECURSE`, `BASE_FILL`, `DONE`, or `UNDEFINED`);
- separate probe and output-chain orders, because gap filling can reorder a probe gap.

At a normal active boundary after a nonzero-height comparison, exactly one current frontier was inspected but not consumed. The other frontier is newly exposed and remains unknown until the next transition. At `H=0`, both candidate absolute imbalances equal one without knowing either sign. This creates two text-compatible operational readings:

- **eager:** inspect both candidate colors, then resolve the observed tie;
- **query-minimal:** observe algebraically that a tie is certain, toss the coin first, and inspect only the selected color.

The source does not specify this reveal-order optimization. The enumerator implements and labels both. At nonzero `H`, both colors are required and the persistent-frontier issue is unavoidable.

### One exact two-block transition

From a chain-step boundary:

1. Retain all previously inspected colors.
2. In eager mode, reveal each active frontier whose color is not yet known. In query-minimal mode at `H=0`, toss the certain-tie coin and reveal only the selected frontier. All reveals branch with the exact hypergeometric conditional probability.
3. Otherwise compare `|H+f(Lfront)|` and `|H+f(Rfront)|`.
4. Choose the strict minimizer, or reveal a fair tie coin and follow it.
5. Consume only the chosen frontier; update `(a,b)`, `C`, `H`, and `D`.
6. Preserve the unchosen inspected frontier in the filtration.
7. Update the last balanced-band visit and start/end an above-band excursion on crossings `1->2` and `2->1`.

This also supplies a micro-filtration at reveal and tie events. The counterexamples below already occur at the coarser chain-step boundaries; no appeal to the still-finer decision-time sigma-algebra is needed.

## 3. Exact conditional law with a persistent frontier

Suppose `h=|H|>=1`, both blocks are active, and the true boundary reveals one persistent frontier. Let `R=n-t` be the number of unassigned elements and let

\[
P=\frac{R-h}{2}
\]

be the number of pool elements whose sign equals `sgn(H)`. This global count identity from the paper is correct. What changes is the conditional sampling law.

- If the persistent frontier has sign `-sgn(H)`, an upward move is impossible, so `p_t=0`.
- If the persistent frontier has sign `sgn(H)`, it already accounts for one of the `P` same-sign elements. The newly exposed other frontier is sampled from `R-1` genuinely unrevealed elements, only `P-1` of which have that sign. Hence
  \[
  p_t=\frac{P-1}{R-1}=\frac{R-h-2}{2(R-1)}.
  \]

This can be nearly `1/2`. The paper's expression `P(P-1)/(R(R-1))` averages over the already-inspected frontier again; it is not the conditional probability on the true history.

For `h=1`, the strict inequality `p_t>1/4` is equivalent to `R>2h+3`, with the parity constraints automatically respected in the family below.

## 4. Eager comparison: smallest Lemma 4.1 counterexample n=8

Let

\[
L=(1,2,3,4),\qquad R=(5,6,7,8).
\]

Consider this positive-probability history:

1. inspect `f(1)=+1` (`1/2`);
2. inspect `f(5)=+1` (`3/7` conditionally);
3. since `H_0=0`, both candidate absolute imbalances are one; the fair tie chooses `L` (`1/2`);
4. consume element `1`.

The true filtration atom has probability

\[
\frac12\cdot\frac37\cdot\frac12=\frac3{28}.
\]

At this boundary, `(a,b)=(1,0)`, `H=1`, `D=1`, element `5` is a known unconsumed `+1` frontier, and element `2` is newly unexposed. Among the six genuinely unexposed values there are exactly two pluses and four minuses. Therefore

\[
\Pr(f(2)=+1\mid\mathcal F_1^{\rm true})=\frac26=\frac13.
\]

If `f(2)=+1`, both frontiers are plus, so every tie outcome raises `h` from one to two. Thus `p_1=1/3>1/4`. The joint probability of the boundary history and the upward event (summing over the second tie coin) is `1/28`. One concrete balanced completion has plus set `{1,2,3,5}`.

The same atom also directly violates the claimed supermartingale calculation:

\[
\mathbb E[3^{h_2}\mid\mathcal F_1^{\rm true}]
=\frac13\,3^2+\frac23\,3^0
=\frac{11}{3}>3=3^{h_1}.
\]

### Exhaustive minimality

The exact enumerator checked every true-filtration atom and every used tie outcome:

| `n` | eligible atoms (`h>=1`, both active) | violating atoms | maximum `p_t` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 0 | 0 | ineligible |
| 4 | 8 | 0 | 0 |
| 6 | 68 | 0 | `1/4` |
| 8 | 556 | 4 | `1/3` |
| 10 | 4,536 | 52 | `3/8` |

Therefore `n=8` is the smallest positive even `n` for failure of Lemma 4.1 under eager comparison and this exact boundary semantics.

### Query-minimal robustness: smallest n=10

The one-step `n=8` atom above uses eager inspection at `H=0`. To avoid resting the failure on that choice, use query-minimal evaluation. For `n=10`, let the first tie choose `L`, reveal and consume `f(1)=+1`. At the next nonzero-height decision both colors must be inspected. Condition on `f(2)=f(6)=+1` and let this new tie choose `L`. This history has probability

\[
\frac12\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac49\cdot\frac38\cdot\frac12=\frac1{48}.
\]

At the next boundary, `(a,b)=(2,0)`, `H=2`, `D=2`, element `6` is a known unconsumed plus, and among seven unexposed elements there are two pluses and five minuses. Hence the next upward probability is `2/7>1/4`. Exact enumeration gives:

| `n` | violating atoms | maximum `p_t` |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 0 | ineligible |
| 4 | 0 | 0 |
| 6 | 0 | `1/10` |
| 8 | 0 | `1/4` |
| 10 | 32 | `1/3` |

Thus `n=10` is the smallest positive even failure under query-minimal evaluation. This robust witness arises only after a nonzero-height comparison, where observing both candidates is necessary.

## 5. Smallest Lemma 3.3 martingale counterexamples

### 5.1 Literal complete path: n=2

Take the balanced coloring `f(1)=+1,f(2)=-1` and let the first tie choose `L`, a history of probability `1/4`. Then `(a,b)=(1,0)` and `D=1`; `L` is exhausted. Definition 3.1 forces the next extension to `R`, so

\[
\mathbb E[D_2-D_1\mid\mathcal F_1^{\rm true}]=-1.
\]

This is the smallest literal counterexample to the sentence that `D(t)` is a martingale along the full length-`m` steered path. The multiscale application stops at first block exhaustion, so the more relevant active-block defect follows.

### 5.2 Both blocks active: n=4

Let `L=(1,2)`, `R=(3,4)` and use the balanced coloring

\[
f(1)=f(3)=+1,\qquad f(2)=f(4)=-1.
\]

At the first step, both candidates have absolute imbalance one. Let the tie choose `L` and consume element `1`. This true-filtration history has probability

\[
\frac12\cdot\frac13\cdot\frac12=\frac1{12}.
\]

At the next boundary, both blocks are active, `(a,b)=(1,0)`, `H=1`, and the right frontier element `3` is a known unconsumed `+1`. Balance forces the newly exposed left frontier element `2` to be `-1`. The builder necessarily chooses `L`, and hence

\[
\mathbb E[D_2-D_1\mid\mathcal F_1^{\rm true}]=1\neq0.
\]

This is exactly where the exchangeability sentence fails: conditional on the true history,

\[
\Pr[(f(2),f(3))=(-1,+1)]=1,
\quad
\Pr[(f(2),f(3))=(+1,-1)]=0.
\]

There is no noninitial active-block boundary at `n=2`, so `n=4` is minimal for the eager active version. The displayed next action exhausts `L`. If one requires a counterexample strictly inside both blocks even after every possible next choice, the eager minimum is `n=6`, with drift `3/4`.

Under query-minimal evaluation, the unchosen step-one color is not inspected, so the eager `n=4` atom is absent. Exact enumeration finds the first active and strict-interior martingale failures at `n=6`, after a nonzero-height decision has necessarily inspected both frontiers. The martingale proof therefore fails under either operational reading.

This falsifies the martingale premise used for the stated Azuma proof. It does **not** by itself falsify the numerical high-probability block-deviation inequality; that inequality could conceivably require a different proof.

## 6. Eager parametric family and independent n=10 reconstruction

Let `n=2k`, `k>=2`. Inspect the first element of each half and condition on both being `+1`; at `H=0`, let the first tie choose `L`. The history probability is

\[
\frac{k}{2k}\frac{k-1}{2k-1}\frac12
=\frac{k-1}{4(2k-1)}
=\frac{n-2}{8(n-1)}.
\]

After consuming `L_1`, the persistent right frontier is a known plus. Among the `2k-2` unexposed elements there are `k-2` pluses and `k` minuses. Hence

\[
p_{\uparrow}=\frac{k-2}{2k-2},
\qquad
\mathbb E[\Delta D]=\frac{k}{2k-2}.
\]

The upward event has joint probability

\[
\frac{k-2}{8(2k-1)}=\frac{n-4}{16(n-1)}.
\]

It violates `p<=1/4` exactly when `k>=4`, proving the `n=8` threshold without relying only on enumeration.

For `n=10`, take halves `(1,...,5)` and `(6,...,10)`, inspect `f(1)=f(6)=+1`, let the first tie choose `L`, and consume `1`. This history has probability `1/9`. There are then three pluses and five minuses among the eight unexposed elements, while element `6` is a known unconsumed plus. Thus

\[
p_{\uparrow}=\frac38,\qquad \mathbb E[\Delta D]=\frac58.
\]

The history-and-upward-event probability is `1/24`. A concrete balanced completion has plus set `{1,2,3,4,6}`. This independently reproduces and strengthens the `n=10` phenomenon by placing it in an all-`n>=8` family and locating the exact minimum.

## 8. Full intended multiscale control flow and where v0 is partial

The source's intended process can be reconstructed as follows.

1. At scale `j`, continue the two-block probe on a contiguous segment `I_j` from saved local indices `(a_0,b_0)`.
2. Record every probe visit to `|H|<=1`. Between consecutive visits, replace the probe order by an order supplied by Lemma 6.1.
3. Stop the scale when either local block is first exhausted. The unconsumed suffix of the other block is the contiguous residual `I_{j+1}`; retain the last balanced visit and the probe tail to exhaustion.
4. If the residual is at least `M0`, split it into halves, run a new two-block probe from `(0,0)` until its first visit to `|H|<=1`, and gap-fill the old tail plus this descent probe. Start scale `j+1` at the descent endpoint `(a',b')`.
5. If the residual is below `M0`, gap-fill the old tail together with the entire residual and finish.

On the true filtration, all frontier colors inspected during the scale probe and descent remain measurable even when the actual output chain reorders the same gap elements. The probe order and the emitted chain order must therefore be separate state components.

Four under-specifications or unjustified transition guards prevent treating the prose as one unique total stochastic machine:

1. **Odd residual.** Definition 3.1 defines the grid for an even segment split
   into equal halves. Block exhaustion can leave an odd residual, while
   Definitions 7.3--7.4 do not impose even nested segment sizes. This occurs
   even in the recursive regime: for `n=2k` with odd `k>=701`, alternate
   `+,-,+,-,...,+` on the left half, give the right half total imbalance `-1`
   with a positive first frontier, and choose left at every zero-height tie.
   The left half exhausts with zero right progress, leaving an odd residual of
   size `k`. No floor/ceiling or padding rule is given.
2. **Gap-filler nondeterminism.** Lemma 6.1 proves existence; when the current imbalance is zero its proof says any element may be chosen. The paper never fixes a canonical filler. Therefore the exact set of emitted intermediate subsets depends on an unspecified choice. Enumerating all valid fillers is a possible completion, but it would be an added convention.
3. **Small initial sizes.** Theorem 8.1 starts with an invariant requiring `m'_j>=M0/2` and says it holds at scale zero because `m'_0=n`. No explicit operational entry case is supplied for `n<M0/2`; asymptotic absorption may repair the theorem statement but is not a defined transition.
4. **Next-scale effective-size guard.** In Case A the proof uses
   `ln m'_j <= (3/2) ln m_{j+1}+1`, said to follow from
   `m_{j+1}>=(m'_j)^(2/3)/2`. The preceding block-deviation event supplies an
   upper bound on the residual, not that lower bound. Even if the missing
   lower bound were assumed, division by two yields the necessary additive
   constant `(3/2)ln 2`, approximately `1.03972`, not `1`; the printed
   implication remains slightly false. The claimed invariant transition is
   therefore not justified as written.

Accordingly, the machine-readable state schema has an explicit `UNDEFINED` phase/reason and models gap filling nondeterministically. This is more faithful than silently choosing an odd split or greedy tie policy. The exact two-block and first-exhaustion state sets are fully enumerable; an “exact multiscale reachable output set” is not uniquely determined by v0 alone.

The schema and transition table are design-level machine-readable
reconstructions, not validators for the enumerator's serialized trace events.
Semantic invariants such as even `n`, equality of the assigned set and order,
and consistency of `H` are checked by executable tests rather than JSON
Schema alone.

Definition 7.4's set system `S` should not be confused with exact process reachability. It takes all valid multiscale decompositions and all local patterns, deliberately over-approximating the sets a chosen execution might emit; distinct decompositions can also represent the same subset.

## 9. What is and is not invalidated

The following conclusions are justified:

- Lemma 4.1 is false on the true reveal filtration: already at `n=8` under eager comparison, and at `n=10` even under query-minimal evaluation.
- `3^{|H_t|}` is not a supermartingale on either true filtration; the displayed eager `n=8` and query-minimal `n=10` atoms are direct witnesses.
- `D_t` is not a martingale on that filtration, even before block exhaustion;
  the smallest eager active witness is `n=4`, while the query-minimal active
  minimum is `n=6`.
- The exchangeability and Azuma arguments given in v0 do not establish their stated downstream bounds.

The following stronger conclusions are **not** justified by this audit:

- that no coarser filtration, coupling, corrected steering rule, or different potential can prove useful imbalance/excursion/deviation bounds;
- that the numerical tail statement of Lemma 3.3 is itself false;
- that no polynomial construction exists;
- that O01 is false or true;
- any complexity-class or algebraic-complexity separation.

A proposed coarse-filtration repair would need to prove both adaptedness and the claimed conditional law after accounting for information encoded by adaptive block choices. Merely deleting the unconsumed color from a written state does not prove it is probabilistically unobserved.
